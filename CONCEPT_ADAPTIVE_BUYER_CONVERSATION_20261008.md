# Adaptive buyer conversation — working concept

Date: 8 October 2026 (Brisbane).
Status: new concept hypotheses for brainstorming, not a claim of invention or authority to build, publish, contact dealers or spend.

## Change in direction

André wants ideas beyond existing marketplace features and an experience that can change on the fly. The website should be an entry into a flexible buyer conversation. The next activity and visual output should respond to the actual problem, and explicit feedback should improve subsequent interactions without waiting for a periodic report.

The earlier platform research remains useful for audience access. Fixed pages, reports and sequential forms should not dictate the engaged buyer's experience.

## Core question

“What has to be true for you to feel ready to choose this car?”

The AI develops a provisional set of buying conditions with the buyer and chooses the next activity that can resolve one. Requirements emerge and change through conversation. It can discover that the blocker is agreement with a partner, total price, equipment certainty, physical fit or choosing among options. It must not assume the first stated need is the true or complete need.

The consistent commercial continuation remains a suitable, permitted Edmunds affiliate/dealer journey. The AI should recommend contact when the buyer has a real question or next step, without manufacturing uncertainty.

## Four mechanisms to develop

### 1. Discover the uncomfortable compromise

Instead of always asking which features the buyer wants, ask which of two plausible sacrifices is less acceptable. For example: pay more for the desired equipment, or keep the budget and give up that equipment. The purpose is to clarify the buyer's preference, not pressure them.

If the buyer rejects both, explore the constraint rather than repeat inventory. If they disagree with the assumptions, correct them. Explain why that answer changes the shortlist.

This is a proposed interaction, not a measured preference-elicitation advantage.

### 2. Maintain provisional buying conditions

Illustrative buyer-agreed conditions:
- Within the buyer's stated total spending limit.
- The essential feature is confirmed on this actual vehicle.
- The household agrees it is a workable choice.
- The buyer is satisfied after a fit check or test drive.

The display can show “known”, “needs checking”, “not met” and “changed by you”. A broad model description or missing catalogue field is not proof of vehicle-level equipment. The buyer can remove or revise a condition at any point.

Instead of presenting a permanent winner, the experience explains “this choice works if these conditions hold” and what would reverse it.

### 3. Switch activity when the blocker changes

A conversation can move from:
- contender comparison;
- to household compromise;
- to a simple buyer-input price comparison;
- to equipment/condition questions;
- to a dealer-ready request;
- back to a revised choice after the buyer supplies a reply.

The AI chooses the relevant activity and display for the next turn. It does not require the buyer to follow a predefined product menu. It can stop showing more cars when more inventory would not help.

Existing catalogue and public Edmunds resources are used where permitted and useful. Access to proprietary calculations, scraping rights, contact automation or appointment APIs is not inferred.

### 4. Bring real-world answers back into the decision

The buyer deliberately contacts the dealer through the applicable Edmunds journey, then can return with a quote, equipment answer or test-drive impression. The AI updates the comparison and next question.

An enquiry is a useful midpoint in the buyer's decision, although an eligible approved enquiry is a commercial outcome for GetCarWise. We should help the person continue rather than automatically end assistance at the outbound click.

No promise is made to send messages, negotiate, fetch private dealer replies or prefill Edmunds forms. Such actions and permissions are outside this concept research.

## Illustrative interaction

Buyer: “Find a hybrid SUV.”
AI: “What would a hybrid improve for you?”
Buyer: “Lower running costs, but I don't drive much.”
AI: offers a comparison of the purchase premium under buyer-supplied usage instead of assuming hybrid is mandatory.
Buyer: “Actually, my partner dislikes older cars.”
AI: updates the problem to a household compromise and presents two supported options with their trade-offs.
Buyer: “We both like this one, if the final price is within our limit.”
AI: explains which costs remain unknown and helps prepare the useful dealer question.
Buyer later supplies the dealer answer.
AI: updates whether the condition is met and adjusts the next step.

This is invented concept illustration; no real listing, price, verified recommendation or current supported product capability is asserted.

## Three kinds of adaptation

1. **Next turn:** explicit buyer responses change priorities, the next question, the display and the useful action immediately.
2. **Returning buyer:** the conversation can retain the buyer's own decision context with appropriate control/consent.
3. **Next buyer:** validated lessons about which question/format helps a particular buying situation can adjust the experience. A buyer's private facts or preferences do not become truths about other people.

For example, if buyers repeatedly say a question about fuel type is premature, the system can try an earlier question about usage or spending. The next buyer may see a different opener. This is a policy-change hypothesis, not evidence that such optimisation already works.

Keep stable the evidence rules, known facts, explicit constraints, consent and supported affiliate destinations. AI can rapidly vary the question sequence, explanatory depth and comparison format. It cannot invent vehicle facts, rewrite approved affiliate terms or publish arbitrary global changes from one person's reaction.

Measurements are inputs to this loop rather than just a retrospective report. Explicit feedback can support immediate local changes. Missing clicks, silence and long conversations are ambiguous; confirm the interpretation when possible. Approved affiliate outcomes arrive later and need their actual reporting delay before broad revenue judgments. Personal adaptation must not optimise for pressure or artificial urgency.

## Novelty check

A targeted current search found first-party descriptions of real-time adaptive automotive guidance:
- Navia describes intent-led, adaptive guidance and result tiles updated through conversation.
- Swirl describes dynamic visual formats, conversation-driven comparisons/calculators and dealer workflows.
- Other indexed offerings include carsales follow-up preference changes and dealer-oriented conversational systems.

Thus adaptive chat or generative interface alone is not an established untouched gap. These are vendor descriptions; none was independently tested here. The research does not establish full competitive coverage, absence of the concept, product quality or commercial results.

The specific working hypothesis is a buyer-directed sequence organised around explicit, revisable buying conditions, an AI-chosen next activity, real-world answers returning into the same decision, and appropriately validated improvements to the next conversation. Components may already exist elsewhere. Distinctiveness must be tested through concrete buyer interactions and a closer comparison of this combination.

Originality can involve a useful new arrangement of known capabilities. Lack of awareness is not proof that nobody has built it.

## Next piece to develop

This makes the proposed behaviour reviewable before architecture or implementation. The first example can start with shortlist rescue but must be able to change tasks when the buyer reveals a different blocker.

The owner's recurring spending ceiling remains $200/month until visible progress; actual model/data costs would need validation before deployment. No new costly platform or independent model training is proposed.
