# GetCarWise SEO/GEO Operating System

**Date:** 2026-09-16  
**Owner lane:** ChatGPT — Business/Strategy  
**Status:** **Proposed — pending André approval before implementation**

## Executive thesis

GetCarWise should not run SEO and GEO as two separate programs. Google's current guidance and GetCarWise's own Search Console data support a single **evidence-first search strategy**:

1. create one strong canonical page for each meaningful shopper intent;
2. make the page easy for humans and search systems to understand and extract;
3. differentiate with current, reproducible GetCarWise market evidence rather than generic listicle copy;
4. make sourcing, methodology, authorship and limitations explicit;
5. connect pages through a clear entity/topic architecture;
6. measure conventional Google Search and Google's Generative AI surfaces separately, using the same underlying page portfolio.

The strongest potential moat is not more words or more schema. It is **current market evidence + transparent decision methodology + trustworthy product/data provenance**.

This strategy is grounded in:

- US-only Google Search Console Search Performance, last 28 days;
- US-only Google Search Console Generative AI features, last 28 days;
- Ubersuggest US SERP and keyword data;
- existing Semrush On-Page SEO Checker, Site Audit, Position Tracking and backlink findings;
- live GetCarWise page/content review;
- current competitor SERPs and page structures;
- current Google Search documentation for people-first content, AI features and structured data.

# 2. The central finding: GEO is not simply "SEO with more words"

The strongest internal contrast is:

### 3-row SUV under $50k

- 41 conventional Search impressions;
- average Search position 19.71;
- **78 Generative AI impressions**.

The page is structurally coherent and highly extractable. It uses consistent vehicle fields, trade-offs, comparison tables, best-use cases, "why buy" and "watch for" sections, buying guidance and FAQs.

### Used PHEV

- **136 conventional Search impressions**;
- average Search position 43.07;
- only **1 Generative AI impression**.

The page is materially thinner and lacks an explicit ranking methodology, comparable evidence fields, current used-market price/mileage evidence, ownership caveats, battery/warranty context and a strong comparison framework.

### SUV under $30k

- a tracked external Ubersuggest signal around **#13** for `best suv under 30000`;
- 21 GSC Search page impressions across its broader query mix;
- only **3 Generative AI impressions**.

The page is already long and useful, so word count is not the issue. It mixes several decision spaces — new subcompact SUVs, used compact SUVs, hybrids/PHEVs — and contains freshness/evidence gaps.

## Working hypothesis

Google's generative surfaces appear more likely to use pages that provide a **coherent answer space with extractable facts, explicit comparisons, trade-offs and entity clarity**. Conventional Search can generate impressions earlier from broad topical relevance even when a page is too weak/deep to win clicks.

This is a hypothesis, not proof of causation. Google's Generative AI report has no query dimension, and the current sample is small. The strategy therefore uses controlled page treatments and repeated measurement rather than assuming a universal GEO formula.

# 3. Why Google's current AI-search architecture matters

Google's current documentation says AI Overviews and AI Mode are rooted in the same foundational Search quality/ranking systems and may use **query fan-out** — issuing multiple related searches across subtopics and data sources.

For GetCarWise, the practical implication is important:

> Build one authoritative page that resolves a shopper's full decision space; do not create a thin page for every keyword or fan-out variation.

A strong page should cover the natural subquestions a buyer asks — price, availability, ownership costs, reliability/risk, fuel/range, warranty, safety, use case, alternatives and trade-offs — while remaining focused on one primary job.

This also supports consolidation of duplicate-intent pages rather than creating more near-duplicates.

# 4. The GetCarWise content moat: live-market evidence

High-authority competitors usually justify their rankings through one of two defensible assets:

- hands-on instrumented testing and editorial expertise; or
- large-scale proprietary market/reliability datasets.

GetCarWise should not imitate testing claims it cannot support. Its defensible advantage can instead be:

> **Current US market evidence combined with a transparent consumer decision framework.**

## Proposed proprietary evidence block

For suitable ranking/decision guides, publish a time-stamped **GetCarWise Market Snapshot** containing reproducible fields such as:

- data date;
- US geography / coverage statement;
- qualifying model years;
- active listing count;
- median asking price;
- median mileage;
- 25th–75th percentile price range where practical;
- meaningful share of listings under the stated budget where practical;
- new/used split where relevant;
- fuel/powertrain availability;
- current Deal Score / ownership-cost / risk fields only when those fields are validated and consistently explainable.

The page must say what the sample represents and what it does not represent.

The data should determine recommendations; recommendations should not be selected first and then supported after the fact.

# 5. Evidence-first ranking/decision page template

This is the proposed standard for future high-value ranking pages. It is a framework, not a requirement that every page have identical wording.

## A. Freshness and identity

- precise H1 aligned to the actual decision intent;
- `Updated` date only when substantive content/data has actually been refreshed;
- `Market data as of` date where live data is used;
- named author and reviewer where appropriate.

## B. 30-second answer

Above the fold or very near it:

- best overall option;
- best alternative by use case;
- key budget/market caveat;
- one-sentence explanation of how the answer was reached.

## C. Market snapshot

Show the current market before presenting opinions.

## D. Transparent methodology

Explain ranking inputs. Candidate dimensions include:

- budget fit/current price;
- current market availability;
- vehicle age/mileage trade-off;
- ownership cost/fuel economy;
- reliability/repair-risk evidence;
- safety/recall context;
- practicality/use-case fit.

Do not publish precise weighting unless the inputs and calculation are reproducible.

## E. Consistent evidence cards

Each candidate should use comparable fields, for example:

- Best for;
- Market price and mileage;
- Availability;
- Relevant fuel/range data;
- Reliability/repair-risk evidence;
- Safety/recall context;
- Warranty/ownership caveat;
- Why buy;
- Watch-outs.

## F. Comparison table

A concise table using the same fields as the evidence cards.

## G. Decision tree

Examples:

- choose X if your priority is lowest purchase price;
- choose Y if fuel cost matters most;
- choose Z if cargo/third-row use matters most.

## H. Caveats and buyer checks

Cover the issues that can invalidate an otherwise attractive ranking: trim differences, warranty transfer rules, battery health, branded titles, recalls, dealer fees, regional pricing and similar material factors.

## I. Sources and methodology notes

Use a visible source hierarchy and state assumptions.

## J. Contextual CarClever action

The CTA should continue the exact decision:

- search current inventory;
- check a specific deal;
- estimate ownership cost;
- compare shortlisted vehicles.

It should not interrupt the informational answer.

## K. Descriptive internal links

Connect to relevant vehicle profiles, Deal Score, title/risk guidance, total-cost content and adjacent budget guides using descriptive anchors.

## L. FAQs

FAQs remain useful for humans and answer coverage, but they should not be created merely to chase FAQ rich results.

# 6. Source and trust standard

## Source hierarchy

For facts used in decision content:

1. **Validated current GetCarWise market data** for current listing/market observations;
2. **Primary sources** — OEMs, EPA, NHTSA, IIHS and other authoritative bodies;
3. strong third-party research where necessary and appropriate;
4. competitor/editorial sources for market research context, not as the main factual foundation of GetCarWise claims.

Time-sensitive claims should include an `as of` context or a clearly current source.

## Critical trust issue found during this research

Public GetCarWise pages are not fully consistent about CarClever's data provenance/capabilities.

Examples requiring product-owner/Claude verification before copy is reconciled:

- the Tools page says CarClever cross-references state title records;
- the current CarClever User Guide says title status comes from dealer-provided listing data where available and is not independently verified;
- Try CarClever contains product-availability/capability wording that appears older than the current User Guide;
- recall/capability wording differs between public surfaces.

A separate spot check also found a material warranty-transfer problem on the high-performing 3-row page: its Kia Sorento discussion treats the 10-year/100,000-mile powertrain coverage as if it protects a used buyer to 100k, while Kia's official warranty documentation states the 120-month/100,000-mile powertrain warranty is an Original Owner warranty and is excluded from normal transfer.

Therefore:

> **P0 before scaling content is a trust/provenance consistency audit.**

A high-GEO page should not be copied mechanically until its factual claims are revalidated.

# 7. Information architecture: one clear job per URL

## Proposed site content types

1. **Hubs / pillars** — `/tools/`, `/data-guides/`, methodology/about/data-source pages;
2. **Ranking / decision guides** — budget/category/use-case pages;
3. **Comparisons** — A vs B vs C pages only where the search intent is genuinely comparative;
4. **Vehicle profiles** — model/year reference pages;
5. **Explainers / reference** — Deal Score, title brands, ownership-cost methodology, etc.;
6. **Product/how-to** — CarClever Guide, Try CarClever, ChatGPT/Claude usage pages.

## Duplicate-intent audit required

The clearest current example is the $25k SUV cluster:

- conventional Search is surfacing `/tools/best-compact-suv-under-25000/`;
- Google's Generative AI page table is surfacing `/tools/best-compact-suv-under-25k-comparison/`;
- the Data & Guides hub visibly contains two similar "Best Compact SUV Under $25K" entries.

This is not sufficient evidence to order a redirect today, but it is enough to require an exact audit of:

- target intent;
- title/H1;
- canonical;
- content overlap;
- query footprint;
- internal anchors;
- backlinks;
- indexing state.

Then either:

- consolidate into one canonical evidence page; or
- make the two page jobs unmistakably different if genuine separate intent exists.

A similar audit is warranted for the 3-row `$50k` and 3-row family/Highlander pages before expanding that cluster.

# 8. Structured data strategy

Structured data should describe visible content accurately; it should not drive the content strategy.

## Proposed rules

- WebSite/site-name markup belongs at the site/homepage level, not on an individual Accord article merely because an SEO tool suggests it.
- Do not use AutoDealer markup for GetCarWise / Deal Score unless GetCarWise actually becomes a local auto dealership entity.
- ItemList can be semantically valid for a list, but do not treat it as the lever that moves an editorial SUV ranking into a supported Google carousel/rich result.
- Keep accurate Organization/Article/Breadcrumb-style markup where appropriate and supported by the site's actual content.
- Consider Dataset markup only if GetCarWise publishes a real accessible dataset meeting Google's requirements; do not create markup for a dataset that users cannot actually access.

Semrush schema ideas remain useful prompts for review, not implementation instructions.

# 9. Controlled case-study design

Do not rewrite the entire site simultaneously. Use a small portfolio that gives different test conditions.

## Treatment A — SUV under $30k

**Role:** near-page-one conventional opportunity; weak GEO visibility.

**Baseline:**
- tracked Ubersuggest head term around #13 for `best suv under 30000`;
- GSC page average position 26.05 across its broader query mix;
- 21 Search impressions;
- 3 Generative AI impressions.

**Treatment:**
- tighten intent;
- correct stale facts;
- add current GetCarWise market snapshot;
- add methodology and source transparency;
- refresh candidates from the actual dataset;
- improve decision structure and descriptive internal links.

## Treatment B — used PHEV

**Role:** highest conventional content-page demand; almost no GEO.

**Baseline:**
- 136 Search impressions;
- position 43.07;
- 1 GenAI impression;
- GSC query `best phev`: 58 impressions / position 38.19;
- GSC query `best used phev cars`: 27 impressions / position 46.41.

**Treatment:** full evidence-led rebuild using used-market price/mileage/availability, electric range, charging/use-case fit, battery/warranty caveats, reliability/repair evidence, comparison table and transparent ranking method.

## Treatment C — $25k SUV cluster

**Role:** high Search demand with possible URL/intent split.

**Baseline:**
- `/under-25000/`: 122 Search impressions / position 38.28;
- separate `/under-25k-comparison/`: 4 GenAI impressions.

**Treatment:** architecture/canonical/query audit **before** content rewrite. Then build one canonical evidence-led page per genuinely distinct intent.

## Treatment D — 3-row SUV under $50k

**Role:** GEO winner / internal positive case study.

**Baseline:**
- 41 Search impressions;
- position 19.71;
- 78 GenAI impressions.

**Treatment:** **protect first**. Do not radically restructure. Perform primary-source accuracy, warranty, freshness, methodology and source QA. Improve factual trust with minimal disruption, then observe.

## Control group

Leave several pages materially unchanged during the first treatment window, for example:

- hybrid SUV under $20k;
- used EV;
- full-size truck towing comparison.

Controls help distinguish treatment effects from site-wide volatility, indexing changes or Google-side shifts.

# 10. Measurement framework

## Pre/post windows

For each treatment URL:

- save 28-day pre-change baseline;
- check 14 days as an early directional read;
- use 28 days as the primary comparison;
- use 56 days as the confirmation window where practical.

Do not overreact to daily fluctuations at current traffic levels.

## URL-level scorecard

Track:

- GSC Search clicks;
- GSC Search impressions;
- CTR;
- average position;
- visible page-filtered query set;
- GSC Generative AI impressions;
- exact target-keyword SERP positions from Semrush/Ubersuggest;
- indexed/canonical state;
- last substantive update date;
- real earned backlinks/referring domains;
- content/source QA status;
- conversions/CarClever actions when traffic becomes sufficient.

Because GSC Generative AI reporting has no query dimension, do not claim that a specific keyword caused AI impressions.

## Recommended additional baseline export

Before major edits to the four treatment areas, export conventional GSC queries with each page filtered separately for:

- last 28 days;
- last 3 months where data volume permits.

This will give a cleaner page-to-query intent map than the current property-wide query export.

Use for:

- US/English Position Tracking;

- On-Page Checker as an idea source;

## Google official documentation / live SERPs

Use to validate:

- feature eligibility;

## GetCarWise/CarClever validated data

Use as the **content evidence engine** — the part competitors cannot copy cheaply.

## GA4 / Clarity

Use later for on-page behavior/conversion diagnostics, but avoid strong conclusions while traffic remains sparse.

# 13. Authority / link-earning strategy

Current Google link data shows near-zero real external authority. Raw Semrush referring-domain growth has been heavily polluted by spam, so link quantity is not a useful success metric.

## Proposed sequence

1. create an original data asset worth citing;
2. publish transparent methodology and a memorable finding;
3. pitch the **finding/data**, not a generic request for a backlink;
4. target automotive editors, personal-finance writers, dealers, enthusiast communities and relevant researchers where the finding is useful;
5. track high-quality earned referring domains and referral traffic, not raw backlink totals.

This turns content production and authority building into one program.

# 14. Site-wide trust/provenance program

Before multiplying evidence-led pages, create one maintained source of truth for public product/data claims.

## Proposed capability/provenance register

For each public claim, record:

- capability;
- current implementation status;
- data source;
- resolution/granularity (listing, VIN, model-year, make/model, etc.);
- what is independently verified vs dealer/provider supplied;
- known limitations;
- approved public wording;
- last validation date.

Apply that wording consistently across:

- homepage;
- Tools;
- CarClever User Guide;
- Try CarClever;
- ChatGPT/Claude pages;
- Deal Score explainer;
- ranking guides where the capability is referenced.

This reduces factual drift and strengthens the trust layer Google explicitly asks publishers to provide through clear sourcing and explanation of how content is produced.

# 17. What not to do

- Do not mass-produce more thin pages around keyword variations.
- Do not rewrite GEO winners simply because another page template looks cleaner.
- Do not keyword-stuff semantic terms from SEO tools.
- Do not add unsupported schema merely because Semrush suggests it.
- Do not chase `llms.txt` or AI-only technical hacks as a visibility strategy.
- Do not count spam referring domains as authority growth.
- Do not claim data provenance, title verification, recalls, warranty coverage or reliability evidence that the actual data contract does not support.
- Do not update a date purely to appear fresh without materially refreshing the page.
- Do not infer a specific AI query from GSC Generative AI impressions; Google currently provides page/country/device/date dimensions, not queries.

# 18. Success definition

## Short term

- move at least one near-page-one decision page into the top 10 for its primary US query cluster;
- increase Search clicks and impressions on treatment pages without degrading cross-surface winners;
- increase GenAI impressions for at least one previously GEO-weak treatment page;
- eliminate duplicate-intent and provenance ambiguity in the priority cluster.

## Medium term

- build multiple pages that earn both conventional and Generative AI visibility;
- establish GetCarWise as a source for current vehicle-market facts, not merely another opinion listicle;
- earn real citations/backlinks to proprietary market assets;
- create repeatable refresh workflows based on live data.

## Strategic end state

GetCarWise should become a **decision-evidence publisher**:

> a site where a shopper can see what the market looks like now, understand how choices were ranked, verify important claims, compare trade-offs, and then use CarClever to act on the decision.

That positioning is simultaneously stronger for users, conventional Search, generative search systems and link earning.

# 19. Known limitations of this research

- GSC Generative AI reporting is new and the current US sample is only 190 impressions across 28 days.
- GSC Generative AI has no query dimension; page-level AI impressions cannot be attributed to a specific keyword.
- Ubersuggest page-level report quota was exhausted during this research; SERP analysis remained available and was used.
- Semrush API units were exhausted; the existing On-Page Checker, engineering report and cached Site Audit evidence were used instead.
- Conventional GSC query exports are privacy-filtered and do not reconcile to all property-level impressions/clicks.
- Page-level GSC average position is an aggregate across queries/devices/result contexts and should not be confused with a single-keyword rank.
- The current case study can identify high-value hypotheses and controlled tests; it cannot prove causal ranking factors from observational data alone.
