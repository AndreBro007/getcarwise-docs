## Replace the current quick-read and total-cost definition with this

### What should a used-car total-cost estimate include?

Include the amount paid for the vehicle, required fees and taxes, financing cost, insurance, fuel or electricity, maintenance and repairs, registration, and an estimate of what the car may sell for later. The result depends on the buyer, location, loan, expected use, and vehicle condition, so show the inputs instead of presenting a generic monthly figure as universal.

The CFPB notes that the total vehicle cost can include the price, optional products, taxes and fees, interest, insurance, maintenance, and fuel. It also advises comparing loan offers using the amount financed, APR, interest rate, term, and payment—not the monthly payment alone. [CFPB: What factors into the price of a car?](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-factors-into-the-price-of-a-car-or-vehicle-en-723/); [CFPB: How do I compare auto loan offers?](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-do-i-compare-auto-loan-offers-what-should-i-look-at-besides-the-monthly-payment-en-753/).

### How can I calculate ownership cost without counting the purchase twice?

Choose one method and use it throughout. A cash-flow calculation can add the down payment, loan payments, fees not included in the loan, running costs, and then subtract the proceeds from a future sale. An economic-cost calculation can add the purchase price, fees, loan interest, and running costs, then subtract estimated resale value. Do not add full loan payments and the full purchase price together.

For either method, disclose whether taxes, add-ons, trade-in value, or a down payment are included. The CFPB lists taxes, titles, fees, add-ons, down payments, trade-in value, interest, and loan term as factors that change the amount paid or borrowed. [CFPB auto-loan affordability guidance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-much-can-i-afford-to-borrow-for-a-car-or-auto-loan-en-751/).

## Replace the current generic insurance, energy, and maintenance estimates with this

### Which ownership-cost inputs should I get for the specific car?

Use a written lender offer for financing, an insurance quote for the exact car and coverage, and expected fuel or electricity use based on how you drive. Check the manufacturer’s maintenance schedule and ask an independent mechanic about work due or concerns on the specific vehicle. Use local tax and registration costs rather than applying a generic monthly estimate.

The CFPB recommends comparing financing terms and considering recurring vehicle costs. The FTC advises buyers to get an independent inspection and request a written report with repair estimates. For vehicle fuel-economy information and a fuel-cost calculator, use the U.S. Department of Energy and EPA’s [FuelEconomy.gov](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/). The NAIC’s [auto-insurance resource](https://content.naic.org/insurance-topics/auto-insurance) provides consumer shopping information and state-level insurance data; an average is background, not a quote for an individual driver and vehicle. [FTC dealer-buying guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/buying-used-car-dealer).

### How should I estimate resale value and depreciation?

Treat future resale value as an estimate, not a guaranteed amount. Start with current market evidence for comparable vehicles and match model year, trim, drivetrain, mileage, condition, and location. Then state how the vehicle’s expected mileage and condition by the planned sale date could change that estimate. Update the estimate if the market or vehicle condition changes.

Current used-car asking prices and price ratings can help establish a present-day comparison, but they do not promise a future transaction price. TrueCar says its used-car Price Rating compares recent local listings for similar vehicles. CarGurus says its IMV and Deal Ratings use all-in prices that include disclosed fees. Cite those as current company methods and keep any future-value assumption separate from those live listing signals. [TrueCar Price Rating FAQ](https://www.truecar.com/faq/); [CarGurus fee-transparency update](https://dealers.cargurus.com/blog/fee-transparency-update).

### How should I present a worked TCO example?

Show the vehicle and the assumptions behind each input: price and fees, financing terms, insurance quote, expected driving, fuel or electricity estimate, maintenance basis, registration and taxes, expected holding period, and estimated resale value. Mark each item as a quote, record, official vehicle figure, or estimate. If an input is unknown, leave it visible as unknown instead of filling it with an invented number.
