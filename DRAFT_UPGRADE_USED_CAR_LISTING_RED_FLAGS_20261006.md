## Replace the price-comparison subsection with this

### How should I compare a listing price with other cars nearby?

Compare vehicles with similar model year, make and model, trim, mileage, condition, equipment, and location. Then compare the complete price, including disclosed mandatory fees. A very low asking price is a prompt to check the listing’s details and history; the price alone cannot establish whether the car is a bargain or a scam.

TrueCar says its used-car Price Rating compares recent listing prices for similar vehicles in the shopper’s area. Its FAQ says the rating does not include dealer documentation, administrative, or similar processing fees. CarGurus says used listings without disclosed fees receive “No Rating” and rank lower in search, and that its IMV and Deal Ratings use all-in prices including disclosed fees. These are company descriptions of different systems, not a shared scale. [TrueCar used-car listings](https://www.truecar.com/used-cars-for-sale/); [TrueCar Price Rating FAQ](https://www.truecar.com/faq/); [CarGurus fee-transparency update](https://dealers.cargurus.com/blog/fee-transparency-update).

Before you travel, ask the dealer for the out-the-door price in writing and confirm that the car is available. The FTC recommends getting written quotes to check advertised prices and identify add-ons or charges that may appear later. [FTC: Buying a Used Car From a Dealer](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/buying-used-car-dealer).

## Replace the title-and-recall check subsection with this

### How can I check a vehicle’s title and recall information?

Ask for the VIN and compare it with the number on the vehicle and its documents. Review title and history records, and search the VIN with NHTSA for unrepaired safety recalls. A lookup or report is one source of evidence, not proof that the car has no problems; arrange an independent inspection of the specific vehicle.

NHTSA explains that a VIN search will not show recalls that have already been repaired and may not show some recently announced recalls before all affected VINs are identified. If a result is unclear, check with the manufacturer or a dealer. [NHTSA recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls).

For another screening step, GetCarWise offers [VIN Check](https://getcarwise.app/tools/vin-check/). Treat any tool output as a lead to verify against the vehicle’s documents and official records; it does not replace a mechanic’s inspection.

## Add this after the existing inspection guidance

### Does a vehicle history report replace an inspection?

No. The FTC says a vehicle history report may show accidents or flood damage but typically will not list mechanical problems, so it is not a substitute for an independent inspection. Ask a mechanic you choose to inspect the actual car and provide a written report tied to its VIN. [FTC: Buying a Used Car From a Dealer](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/buying-used-car-dealer).

For dealer purchases, read the Buyers Guide and make sure the warranty or “as is” terms match the paperwork. The FTC says the Guide identifies warranty status, tells buyers to get promises in writing, and encourages an independent inspection. The FTC’s separate [Buying a Used Car - Consumer Tips video and transcript](https://consumer.ftc.gov/media/79915) also covers the Buyers Guide, history reports, inspection, and recalls.

### What should I verify before sending a deposit or signing?

Get the VIN, the full written price, the deposit terms, and the seller’s answers about title, warranty, and condition. Confirm the vehicle is available and inspect it before paying when possible. If the seller changes the price, adds unexplained charges, or will not allow reasonable checks, pause and reassess.

For a dealer purchase, compare the written out-the-door quote with the listing and ask for every proposed add-on to be itemized. The FTC advises buyers to request written out-the-door prices before visiting, review the Buyers Guide, and get an independent inspection. Keep copies of the listing and the seller’s written statements. [FTC dealer guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/buying-used-car-dealer); [FTC online used-car guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2024/07/what-know-when-buying-used-car-online).
