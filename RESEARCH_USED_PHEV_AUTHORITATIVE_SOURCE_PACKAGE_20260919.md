# 3. Toyota Prius Prime

## Why it belongs

Claude's provider pool was the largest of the tested PHEVs at roughly **940 used listings**.

Toyota's 2024 documentation gives:

- **SE:** up to **44 miles** all-electric range;
- **XSE / XSE Premium:** **39 miles** all-electric range;
- **SE:** up to **52 MPG combined** in hybrid mode;
- **XSE/XSE Premium:** **48 MPG combined**.

The key editorial advantage is not merely EV range. Prius Prime is unusually efficient **after the battery is depleted**, which matters for buyers who cannot keep every trip inside the EV window.

## Warranty

Toyota states:

- hybrid battery: **10 years / 150,000 miles** from original date of first use;
- hybrid-related components: generally **8 years / 100,000 miles**;
- Toyota's 2024 Prius Prime/RAV4 Prime press material explicitly says the hybrid battery coverage is **transferable across ownership**.

## Sources

Primary:
- Toyota 2024 Prius Prime newsroom: https://pressroom.toyota.com/vehicle/2024-toyota-prius-prime/
- Toyota detailed 2024 Prius Prime release: https://pressroom.toyota.com/unplug-and-play-2024-toyota-prius-prime-redefines-the-daily-drive/
- Toyota 2024 warranty guide: https://assets.sia.toyota.com/publications/en/omms-s/T-MMS-24PriusPrime/pdf/T-MMS-24PriusPrime.pdf
- Toyota battery warranty: https://www.toyota.com/electrified-vehicles/warranty/

### Editorial role

**Best fit:** commuter / efficiency-first buyer who can charge regularly but still drives long distances.

**Watch-out:** trim matters. Do not publish "44 miles / 52 MPG" as if every 2024 Prius Prime trim gets both figures.

# 6. Mitsubishi Outlander PHEV

## Why it belongs

Claude's provider pool: roughly **315**.

2024 evidence:

- **38 miles** EV range;
- **26 MPG combined** after charge depletion;
- AWD;
- seven seats.

This creates a very different trade-off from Prius/RAV4/Escape:

> strong EV utility + AWD + occasional third-row capability, but much weaker gasoline-mode efficiency.

That is exactly the sort of trade-off the rebuilt page should expose.

## Warranty nuance

Mitsubishi's 2023 Outlander PHEV warranty manual states at the front that the warranty is provided to the **original and subsequent owner(s)**.

It separately provides:

- Main Drive Lithium-ion Battery Coverage: **10 years / 100,000 miles**;
- 10-year/100,000-mile Powertrain Coverage: explicitly **original owner only**;
- subsequent owners receive the balance of the 5-year/60,000-mile basic/powertrain coverage for the separate powertrain benefit.

This distinction is important. Do not collapse the battery warranty and powertrain warranty into one "10/100 warranty" statement.

# 7. Kia Sportage Plug-In Hybrid

## Why it belongs

Claude's provider pool: roughly **257**.

Kia's 2024 material gives:

- **34 miles** all-electric range;
- **84 MPGe** combined electric+gas metric;
- standard AWD.

Kia's 2024 media release gives **35 MPG combined** gasoline-mode EPA estimate.

Independent EPA-spec aggregation also gives 34 EV miles / 35 MPG combined after battery depletion.

## Warranty — publication caution

Kia's U.S. HEV/PHEV warranty guide structure lists:

- Hybrid System: **10 years / 100,000 miles**;
- Powertrain: 10 years / 100,000 miles for the **original owner**, with subsequent-owner powertrain coverage reducing to 5 years / 60,000 miles.

The publicly indexed Kia owner page promotes the general 10/100 program but does not expose enough model-specific transfer nuance on one simple page.

### Publication rule

Until the exact 2023/2024 Sportage PHEV U.S. warranty guide is checked directly at publication time, write:

> Kia provides long hybrid-system coverage, but used buyers should verify the exact VIN/model-year warranty. Kia's full 10-year/100,000-mile powertrain term is an original-owner benefit and does not transfer in full to an ordinary subsequent owner.

Do **not** say simply "10-year/100k warranty transfers."

# 8. Chrysler Pacifica Plug-In Hybrid

## Why it belongs

Claude's provider pool: roughly **623**, second-highest in the tested group.

2024 evidence:

- **32 miles** EV range;
- **30 MPG combined** once battery is depleted;
- FWD;
- seven seats.

It is uniquely valuable for families because no compact-SUV alternative in this set matches a minivan's passenger/cargo use case.

Chrysler has since discontinued the Pacifica Plug-In Hybrid, which makes it explicitly a **used-market** proposition.

## High-voltage battery warranty

The 2024 Chrysler warranty manual states:

- **8 years / 100,000 miles** in states that do not adopt the specified California/TZEV rules;
- **10 years / 150,000 miles** in qualifying California/TZEV states listed in the manual.

Do not publish one national battery-warranty term without the state qualification.

## Material recall/watch-out

This model needs a stronger safety caveat than the other five.

NHTSA recall **26V362** (June 2026) covers certain **2020–2022 Chrysler Pacifica PHEV** vehicles because the traction battery may cause a vehicle fire even while parked.

NHTSA's remedy instructions include no charging and parking outside/away from structures until the affected vehicle is repaired.

Earlier recall **24V536** also concerned Pacifica PHEV HV-battery fire risk and superseded an earlier ineffective remedy for affected vehicles.

### Publication rule

Do not say "Pacifica PHEVs are unsafe."

Say:

> Certain Pacifica PHEV model years have been subject to serious high-voltage battery fire recalls. Check the exact VIN in NHTSA's recall database and confirm all remedies are complete before buying.

- 2024 Chrysler warranty manual: https://vehicleinfo.mopar.com/assets/publications/en-us/Chrysler/2024/Pacifica/5544369_24_C_GW_EN_US_DIGITAL_E1.pdf
- NHTSA recall 26V362: https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2026/RCAK-26V362-7113.pdf

**Best fit:** family buyer who genuinely needs minivan space and can verify recall completion.

**Watch-out:** VIN-level recall verification is mandatory advice, not optional fine print.

# 10. Page-wide PHEV buying rule

The article should explain one core economic/behavioral reality:

> **PHEVs deliver the most value when the owner actually plugs them in.**

Why:

- Prius Prime / RAV4 Prime / Escape can remain efficient after battery depletion.
- Outlander and Pacifica use materially more gasoline once the EV range is exhausted.
- Therefore a buyer's home/work charging access and daily-trip length change which model is genuinely "best."

Recommended first decision question:

> **Can you charge at home or work most days, and is your normal daily driving within roughly 30–45 miles?**

If **yes**, compare PHEVs.

If **no**, explicitly tell the reader to compare a conventional hybrid too.

That creates useful, independent advice instead of affiliate-shaped content.

# 11. Model-year traps the page must explain

## Prius Prime

2023+ is the newer generation with the large range/performance step.

Do not blend old-generation range figures with 2023+.

## RAV4 Prime

Recent model years remain broadly similar in the core 42-mile/38-MPG proposition, but verify year-specific equipment/charging details if discussed.

## Pacifica PHEV

Recall status is **VIN/model-year critical**. Do not make a generic model-level "recall fixed" assertion.

## Outlander PHEV

2023+ is the redesigned generation with the 38-mile U.S. EV-range proposition. Older used Outlander PHEVs should not inherit 2023+ range/cabin claims.

## Sportage PHEV

Introduced with the current generation for 2023 in the U.S.; verify exact warranty guide and year before publishing transfer claims.

## Escape PHEV

FWD is a key model constraint. Do not imply AWD availability on the PHEV.
